A rail map driven by a small data contract

Assembled hardware + published service

01My contribution

A custom Sydney rail-map PCB and a service that converts TfNSW vehicle positions into LED states.

02The constraint

Make real-time rail activity readable on a 291-pixel PCB, with the copper, solder mask and silkscreen forming the map itself.

03Design decisions

The service builds station-and-route keys from static GTFS data and reduces realtime vehicle positions to a compact set of LED states for the Wi-Fi-connected board.

Feed caching, per-feed retry backoff and last-good-data expiry keep the update path bounded when an upstream feed fails or rate-limits requests.

04Evidence & validation

The photographs show the assembled board and illuminated routes. The public service repository includes tests for feed caching, backoff and stale-data expiry.

05Current outcome

A 300 × 305.7 mm physical rail display with public service and firmware source. Its PCB-as-map approach later informed the smaller TramTrace layout.
