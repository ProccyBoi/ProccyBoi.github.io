# Tucson Mountain District: Projects hero terrain

The ridgeline is modeled, not photographed. This local, self-hosted elevation grid is centered on 32.217° N, 111.153° W, looking east-northeast (67° true) toward the Tucson Mountains in southern Arizona. It is a landscape setting for the trainer, **not a survey or representation of its competition runway**. The low field, strip, vegetation, material and lighting are a designed interpretation. The real trainer CAD and its original animations have not been regenerated.

**Elevation source:** Mapzen [Terrain Tiles on AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/), Terrarium zoom 11. United States 3DEP (formerly NED) and global GMTED2010 and SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey. [Dataset compilation and attribution](https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md).

* `relief.bin`: 193 × 185 unsigned 16-bit little-endian orthometric heights, nearest metre. Rows are 150 m apart along the viewing bearing, starting 3,000 m behind the origin; columns are 150 m apart toward bearing 157°, starting 14,400 m to the left. The binary is 71,410 bytes. Distant relief is reduced uniformly in all three axes; the foreground is graded flat to meet the wheels of the metre-scale aircraft.
* `surface.webp`: geographic **material data**, not a background or aerial photograph. Red stores directional hill illumination and green stores local ridge convexity, computed from the elevation source sampled at 37.5 m. It is UV-mapped onto the world-space geometry; it does not contain a sky, vegetation, aircraft or scene composition.
* Rebuild with Python, NumPy and Pillow: `python scripts/build-tucson-relief.py`. Optionally pass a persistent tile cache as the first argument. No tile service is contacted by the website.
* Field references: [NPS geology](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/learn/nature/geology.htm) guided the Tucson Mountains' volcanic bedrock and gravel bajada; [NPS Gates Pass](https://www.nps.gov/places/gates-pass-trailhead.htm) guided the relief and low-sun light; the [USGS rocky-slope reference](https://www.usgs.gov/media/images/saguaro-cacti-a-rocky-slope-sonoran-desert-az) informed cactus silhouettes and spacing.

The landscape adds four draw calls, uses one deterministically generated grain texture, and has no animation loop or additional WebGL context. Static frames are captured from the same live scene with the same CAD; they are not independently composed.
