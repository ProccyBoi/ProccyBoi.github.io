F110211 diagnostic

Flight log checks from 11 July 2026, including aircraft logging, the ground link, sensor validity and changes made after the flight.

What worked

The BNO08x, BME280, GNSS, aircraft SD card, SX1262 air link and ground-station logging all worked during the flight.

The recorded BNO attitude is valid but uses the side-mounted PCB frame. The aircraft navigation EKF diverged; the replay therefore uses raw GNSS for horizontal position and barometric height for its default vertical path.

Aircraft log

  • 13,773 binary records were recovered; 13,772 contain the complete fields required by the visualiser.
  • 679.331 seconds of data at an actual average rate of 20.27 Hz.
  • BNO data was usable on every normalised aircraft row, with no BNO resets.
  • BME altitude ranged from 123.11 to 260.38 m. Temperature ranged from 19.73 to 28.52 °C.
  • GNSS maintained a fix with 6 to 16 satellites and a median horizontal accuracy estimate of 1.18 m.
  • Peak GPS ground speed was 37.98 m/s (136.7 km/h).
  • The horizontal ground track covered 6,785.42 m.

Ground link

  • 8,741 packets were logged over 678.384 seconds, averaging 12.88 received packets per second.
  • RSSI ranged from −106 to −33 dBm, with a median of −82 dBm.
  • SNR ranged from −3.75 to 9.75 dB, with a median of 8.25 dB.
  • The receiver logged a complete recovery track.
  • Ten hertz remains the preferred live-flight setting until controlled range testing supports a higher rate.

Invalid or missing data

  • The aircraft EKF position and velocity are not valid for this flight.
  • Digital and analogue airspeed channels were not valid.
  • The HX711 showed the expected unloaded or saturated state.
  • No useful ESC telemetry was present.

Replay and notes

The public replay includes the 3D route, GPS map, synchronised charts, attitude display, auto-level controls and the exact recorded GPS track.
