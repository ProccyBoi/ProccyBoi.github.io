Small enough to hold.
Deep enough to explore.

Kiku brings industrial design, a custom circuit board and embedded software into one physical object. A rotary encoder and two keys handle the everyday interactions; underneath, an STM32 coordinates storage, a radio tuner, a Bluetooth receiver, a stereo codec, a speaker amplifier and battery management. This is the project from the outside in.

02 / Follow the signal

Three ways in.
One audio system.

Each source starts differently. Local files become decoded PCM. Bluetooth arrives over a dedicated I²S interface. FM begins as analogue audio and returns through the codec’s ADC. The firmware brings them into a common 48 kHz output domain.

The SDMMC peripheral reads a FAT filesystem. WAV and MP3 decoding feed a stereo FIFO; local playback is prefetched before display work. Supported WAV input is 16-bit mono or stereo at 8–96 kHz, converted to the 48 kHz output domain.

TLV320AIC3104 → 3.5 mm headphonesMAX98357A → mono speaker

Choose where it plays.

Output selection offers Auto, Headphones, Speaker and Both. Auto uses the switched 3.5 mm jack. This is a separate decision from the listening source, so changing between radio and SD does not mean changing the whole output system.

Bluetooth has a direction.

Kiku receives audio from a phone through the BM83. This board does not transmit radio or SD music to Bluetooth headphones. The required BM83 input connections are absent; enabling that would require a hardware revision and compatible transmitter firmware.

The silicon behind the experience.

Every major block has a job. The integration is the project.

01

Control + scheduling

STM32H523VET6

Cortex-M33 configured at 160 MHz, with a 512 KiB flash / 272 KiB RAM linker layout. FreeRTOS runs the application task while circular DMA and interrupt callbacks sustain the audio stream; TIM6 supplies the HAL timebase.

02

Bluetooth receiving

Microchip BM83

A2DP sink, with UART host control at 115,200 baud and a separate I²S2 PCM path. Playback commands, connection events and metadata need to remain coherent across source changes.

03

Radio

Silicon Labs SI4705-D60

FM tuning and seek, with RDS station names and RadioText. Analogue left and right channels feed the codec ADC before joining the digital output path.

04

Conversion + listening

TLV320AIC3104 + MAX98357A

A stereo audio codec and headphone driver work alongside a mono I²S speaker amplifier. Bluetooth’s incoming 24-bit samples are reduced to 16-bit PCM in the current stream path.

05

Power + battery

BQ25895 + MAX17048

Power-path charging and fuel gauging are separate functions. USB-C provides power and detection. USB data lines are not routed to the MCU, so firmware programming uses SWD and files reach the player on the SD card.

06

Display + storage

ST7789 + 4-bit SDMMC

A 2-inch HS20HS072RX display runs in 320 × 240 landscape. The current library holds up to 64 tracks. A push encoder and two keys keep the interface usable without a touchscreen.

04 / The shape of the decisions

Designed, printed,
reconsidered.

The soft exterior hides a tightly constrained assembly. The important design changes came from making room for the battery, protecting a delicate display connection and finding out what a physical print could actually tolerate.

Form / first print

A pebble with a purpose.

Mame’s soft body was selected from three form studies: the pebble-like Mame, an offset Nagare shape and the smaller capsule-like Mini. Keeping the PCB datums fixed made the comparison about the enclosure rather than moving the electronics to flatter a particular silhouette.

Review of P1 identified practical issues: a fragile 0.52 mm divider between ports and seam locators that protruded outside the intended envelope. P2 combines the lower USB and headphone recess and moves the locating features inside.

Packaging / service

A stand, a cell
and an antenna.

The P2 battery bay grows from P1’s 35 × 51 mm to 45 × 61 mm. Its nominal 11.4 mm depth gives a 10 mm reference cell about 1.4 mm of headroom. A separate guard and a defined cable route keep battery packaging visible as an engineering constraint.

The antenna nests upward along the right rear edge, reducing the collapsed assembly width by about 15.5 mm relative to P1. The folding U-frame stand adds depth, uses M3 hinges and 6 × 2 mm magnets for folded retention, and incorporates 45° mechanical stops. The resulting desk screen angle is a CAD calculation; physical stability still needs checking.

Display / assembly

The hardest part
is the little flex.

The 2-inch display sits in a removable four-screw cradle. Three sides locate it with about 0.4 mm nominal side clearance, while compliant support avoids treating the optical assembly like a structural plate. The flex exits through an open side and follows a U-fold studied at roughly 4.3 mm bend radius.

A 54 × 37.8 × 1 mm clear lens protects the front. The optical assembly was moved by 0.615 mm to line up with the connector geometry. Those small changes matter: the 20.7 mm flex extension and the supplier’s contact/stiffener dimensions do not completely agree with the connector’s recommended insertion geometry. The CAD documents the fit study; a real mating check remains necessary.

More about antenna packaging +

The antenna reference is listed as 105 mm collapsed, 310 mm extended and 9 mm in diameter. Some hinge and rod dimensions used for packaging came from image estimates, so they are fit assumptions rather than measured parts. The P2 study uses a 100 mm coax pigtail along a 90.6 mm nominal route with 4 mm bend radii and checks metal clearance against a 15 mm target. These are geometric checks; they do not establish antenna efficiency, reception quality or RF certification.

05 / The invisible engineering

The music has
a deadline.

Audio cannot wait for a redraw, a slow file read or a Bluetooth metadata exchange. Much of Kiku’s firmware work is about protecting that timing while making the controls feel immediate.

~171 ms8,192-frame stereo FIFO at 48 kHz
~128 msLocal playback prefill target

Audio / scheduling

Buffer first.
Draw second.

The DMA buffers cover 4 ms of audio, split into 2 ms service windows. A larger stereo FIFO absorbs some timing variation between data producers and the output. Local playback fills ahead before display work begins, giving the audio engine room to keep running.

The current resampler uses nearest-neighbour conversion. Bluetooth clock drift is handled with elementary frame dropping or duplication. These are explicit implementation trade-offs, not claims of bit-perfect output or transparent sample-rate conversion.

Two masks. Ten looks. Less bus traffic. +

The UI keeps current and previous frames in two 9,600-byte one-bit masks. A theme palette translates those masks into RGB565 pixels. Comparing the masks yields a dirty bounding rectangle, so the display transfer can cover only the changed region; an unchanged state needs no redraw.

This gives ten visual treatments a shared rendering foundation. The browser player above compiles that same renderer, while the project’s capture checks compare complete frames against dirty-region updates.

Acknowledged is not the same as finished. +

A Bluetooth command acknowledgement does not mean its metadata transaction has completed. Releasing ownership too early can confuse a late response with a new request, particularly when leaving and returning to the source.

The firmware retains ownership until the matching response or an appropriate profile boundary. Transport controls remain available even when metadata stalls. This separates “the module heard me” from “the data exchange is complete.”

A wake-up bug hidden inside a timestamp. +

Restoring hardware during wake can block long enough to invalidate an earlier timestamp. An unsigned elapsed-time calculation then made newly changed settings appear ready to save immediately.

Resampling time after restoration preserves the intended delayed write. It is a small correction with consequences for responsiveness, storage traffic and predictable settings persistence.

Settings that survive more than a happy path. +

Settings use two CRC-protected records. The backup is written before the primary; records are validated on read, and failed saves back off rather than retrying continuously.

The look selection occupies a previously reserved byte, preserving the 14-byte version-one payload layout. Persistence still depends on having a writable SD card; the interface works without one, but it cannot promise a durable settings save.

06 / Show the work

Evidence, down
to the pixels.

The latest documented firmware refresh is dated 12 October 2026. Build checks, renderer captures and on-device observations establish different things; this record keeps those distinctions visible.

Renderer

130 real captures

Ten looks across the captured screens and states. Bounds checks and full-frame versus dirty-region equivalence passed, checking the rendering behaviour as well as its appearance.

Firmware

Clean build + host tests

The documented firmware refresh passed host tests and clean build checks. Its binary image size was 154,836 bytes. Artifact hashes and readback comparison connect the built image to the flashed device.

Device

Flash verified

Populated HEX ranges matched flash readback. Boot observations reported no application or DMA errors. That verifies the tested boot and image, rather than long-term playback under every condition.

Resources

Static stack gate

The largest reported first-party per-function static frame was 16,616 bytes, below a 24 KiB gate. Static compiler reports do not measure the maximum live call chain; runtime stack headroom remains a separate concern.

A prototype with a clear next test.

Kiku’s firmware and electronics are working development hardware. Mame P2 is the latest enclosure study, informed by a printed P1. The remaining work is concrete:

  • Bluetooth soak: the observed BM83 firmware is 1.2, below the project’s 1.3+ production requirement. Metadata recovery needs a longer run; a short FIFO overflow was observed around wake.
  • Power validation: battery voltage readings disagree between the gauge and charger. State of charge, final charging policy and battery runtime are not yet validated.
  • Listening checks: physical SD insertion/removal, headphone switching, acoustic output and extended playback still need a complete bench pass. The available FM reception check was weak.
  • Mechanical fit: print P2, qualify the display flex and connector, check the real battery and antenna, then assess stand stability and assembly clearances.
