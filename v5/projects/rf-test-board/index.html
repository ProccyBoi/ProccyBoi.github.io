01 / The work

A physical check on the RF layout

Fabricated · VNA evaluation

My contribution

A two-layer RF comparison board, ordered without impedance control and measured with a vector network analyser.

The task

Check calculated feedline geometry against the inexpensive 1.6 mm FR-4 fabrication process used for the preceding LoRa receiver.

Design decisions

Place masked and unmasked coplanar traces, u.FL and SMA transitions, tuning sections and split-pi footprints on the same board.

Print the stack-up and nominal trace widths on the reverse: 1 oz copper, a 0.2 mm ground gap, and widths of 0.8877 mm with mask and 1.0244 mm without it. The design target is 50 Ω.

What has been checked

VNA evaluation is documented in the project record. Photographs identify the comparison structures and the printed stack-up and geometry.

Constraints and challenges

The earlier LoRa receiver's Rev A feedline missed the intended 50 Ω impedance. Investigating it with a VNA led to this dedicated comparison board.

Result and next step

A reusable physical reference for comparing feedlines, connector transitions and filter footprints on the chosen fabrication process.

Read the detailed project notes

Test setup

The useful RF advice is usually short: control the impedance, keep the return path intact, and place the matching network close. What I wanted to know was how much each detail changed the result on the inexpensive two-layer process I was actually using.

I ordered this board without impedance control and measured it with a VNA. That gave me a physical reference for the manufacturer's stack-up instead of another calculator result.

What I compared

The board carries straight coplanar traces, tuning-capacitor sections and split-pi filter footprints, with both u.FL and SMA transitions. It also repeats the feedline with and without solder mask.

The target was 50 Ω on 1.6 mm FR-4 with 1 oz outer copper and a 0.2 mm gap to the surrounding copper. The nominal trace widths printed on the back are 0.8877 mm with mask and 1.0244 mm without it. Keeping those numbers on the board means the sample stays useful after the design files have disappeared into an archive.
