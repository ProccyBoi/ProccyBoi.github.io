Live transport display

TramTrace

A 100 × 200 mm map that puts one RGB LED at every stop on Sydney and Parramatta's light-rail network.

Network
L1, L2, L3 and L4
Controller
ESP32-WROOM-32E
Display
WS2812C-2020 RGB LEDs
Board
100 × 200 mm, Rev 1.0

Metroboard's sibling

Metroboard covered Sydney's heavy-rail network, so the obvious follow-up was the tram network. TramTrace squeezes the CBD and Parramatta lines onto a smaller board without turning the route into an unreadable knot.

The board joins Wi-Fi and gets vehicle positions from a small service I wrote. The LEDs then turn those positions into something I can read at a glance from across the room.

On the board

An ESP32-WROOM-32E handles the network connection and display logic. A CH340C gives me a straightforward USB programming path, while level shifting keeps the ESP32 and LED data happy together.

The hard part was physical rather than computational: the station names, route geometry and components all had to share the same 100 × 200 mm rectangle. The silkscreen ended up doing real work as part map, part assembly drawing.
