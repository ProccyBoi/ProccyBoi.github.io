Source-derived 3D

Inside the RGB coaster.

The enclosure halves come from the supplied STEP files and the fitted parts come from the KiCad-exported assembly, with passives and support packages kept as distinct source-derived groups. The black board carries the real KiCad mask openings—including the HALO artwork—plus front and back silkscreen. The four SWD pogo targets stay with the PCB because there is no fitted 1×4 connector. Use the drink simulator to place a procedural cup or Coke-can mockup: simulated VEML7700 and SHT4x readings then drive the LED response. The drink objects are visualiser aids, not source CAD.

Detect the drink

The VEML7700 sits near the centre of the board and turns the cup's shadow into a placement signal. At startup the firmware learns the uncovered light level for two seconds. A drop to 65% or less of that reference for 500 ms marks the coaster occupied; light returning to at least 85% for 500 ms marks it empty again.

That makes the same sensor useful for both presence and brightness without adding a switch or load cell. The trade-off is optical: room-light changes, clear cups and RGB reflections through the lid can all affect the reading, so the firmware uses hysteresis and minimum-light thresholds rather than treating every dip as a cup.

Measure the change

An SHT4x tracks local temperature under the centre of the coaster. The sensor is separated from the cup by the enclosure: the clear lid is 3 mm thick and the CAD leaves about 2.7 mm between the package and the lid. The firmware therefore displays change at the PCB sensor instead of pretending it knows the drink's actual temperature.

While the coaster is empty, its temperature reference follows the room slowly. When a cup is placed, that reference freezes and small local changes shift the colour from cool tones through neutral to warm tones. Temperature and light are sampled four times per second while the LED frame updates at 40 fps.

Firmware with two moods

The default build keeps a calm six-second travelling highlight around the ring. A separate Drink Companion build adds a rainbow welcome, cool glints, warm embers, an aurora and a goodbye sparkle while keeping the same sensor and fault logic.

Both current images have been flash-verified on the assembled board. The fun build at source commit 9da0988 was also observed running at about 3.26 V with temperature, humidity, ambient-light, WHITE-channel and LED-frame counters advancing without recorded sensor or LED-transfer errors. Cup behaviour through the clear resin and the final thermal response still need physical tuning.
