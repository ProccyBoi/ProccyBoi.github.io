Inside the design

A second record at the field

The crew needs telemetry and logging controls without depending on a laptop, plus a separate record if the aircraft log becomes inaccessible.

Store valid received packets locally as CSV or indexed, CRC-protected binary. Expose link and sensor status on the OLED, and provide a phone-ready Wi-Fi dashboard and pre-flight checklist.

The published build photographs show the receiver, antenna connection and microSD hardware. The documented workflow adds a rate-limited USB stream for another record on a laptop.

A ground log contains the received live subset, not the full aircraft log. No packet-loss benchmark or damaged-record recovery test result is published here.

Ground station

Ground station receiver

I developed the ground station around an ESP32-WROOM-32E, SX1262 radio, microSD socket and a Qwiic connector for the OLED. Two buttons and two activity LEDs keep the common field actions available even when nobody has a laptop open.

Every valid aircraft packet can be stored locally, so the team gets an independent copy of the live data even if the aircraft is damaged or its card is hard to reach. The board can write readable CSV or an indexed, CRC-protected binary format that can recover past a damaged record.

Field workflow

The OLED cycles between an artificial horizon, link figures and sensor status. It shows RSSI, GPS and SD state, heading, satellite count and IMU accuracy instead of reducing the whole system to a single “connected” light.

The ESP32 also creates a Wi-Fi access point with a captive dashboard and pre-flight checklist. From a phone, the crew can inspect fresh telemetry, name a flight, choose the log format, add a marker or change the radio profile. Start and stop commands are repeated over LoRa so the aircraft and ground logs begin together.

Aircraft, receiver and laptop logs

The receiver streams a rate-limited 230400-baud feed over USB without slowing the SD logger. A PC dashboard can record that stream as another CSV backup, while the onboard dashboard remains available when the laptop is not.

Giving the aircraft, receiver and laptop separate records costs some storage, but it has made flight-day problems much less mysterious.
